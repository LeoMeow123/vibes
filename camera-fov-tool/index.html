Quick Verdict for Animal Tracking
DJI Action 5 Pro
Best all-round choice for behavioral experiments. Reliable app with synchronized preview, no overheating, long battery. Great for top-down recording where 4K is sufficient. Affordable.
GoPro HERO 13
Highest raw resolution (5.3K) but undermined by poor app reliability (50% connection failure), overheating at 4K that cuts recordings, and sluggish remote control. Best as backup or for short clips.
Basler ace 2
Gold standard for precision tracking. Global shutter eliminates motion blur, 165fps continuous, hardware trigger for multi-camera sync. Monochrome only but higher effective resolution per pixel. Requires PC + 5GigE infrastructure.

1. Field of View (FOV)

FOV is the angular extent of the scene a camera can capture. It depends on two physical properties: the sensor size and the focal length of the lens.

FOV = 2 × arctan( sensor_size / (2 × focal_length) )

A larger sensor or shorter focal length gives a wider FOV. Action cameras use very short focal lengths (~2–3mm) to achieve ultra-wide views (>150°). Machine vision cameras use interchangeable lenses, so you choose the FOV by selecting the appropriate focal length.

Horizontal FOV (HFOV)

Uses sensor width in the formula. Determines how wide the scene is captured left-to-right.

DJI Action: ~142° · Basler+6mm: ~87°

Vertical FOV (VFOV)

Uses sensor height. Determines top-to-bottom coverage.

DJI Action: ~110° · Basler+6mm: ~61°

2. Distance & Coverage

Once you know the FOV, you can calculate how much area the camera covers at a given distance, or conversely, how far away you need to mount the camera to see a given area.

coverage = 2 × distance × tan( FOV / 2 )
distance = object_size / ( 2 × tan( FOV / 2 ) )

For a T-maze that is 120cm wide, a DJI Action (HFOV ~142°) needs only ~18cm distance to cover it horizontally. A Basler with 6mm lens (HFOV ~87°) needs ~57cm.

Important: You usually want 10–20% margin around the object, so multiply the minimum distance by 1.1–1.2.

3. Pixel Density (Ground Sampling Distance)

Pixel density tells you how many pixels represent each centimeter of your scene. Higher pixel density means finer detail (better for keypoint tracking).

GSD = coverage / resolution   (cm per pixel)
pixel_density = resolution / coverage   (pixels per cm)

For SLEAP pose estimation, you typically want at least 3–5 pixels per body part width. For a mouse paw that is ~5mm wide, you need GSD ≤ ~1.5mm/px, which means pixel density ≥ ~7 px/cm.

4. Sensor Size & Pixel Size

Larger pixels collect more light, producing cleaner images in dim conditions. The Basler IMX392 has 5.86µm pixels — much larger than the DJI's ~2.4µm or GoPro's ~1.55µm. This makes the Basler significantly better for low-light and IR-illuminated experiments.

Monochrome advantage: Color sensors use a Bayer filter that assigns each pixel to R, G, or B, effectively reducing spatial resolution by ~2×. The Basler's monochrome sensor uses every pixel at full resolution, giving it an effective spatial resolution advantage despite its lower pixel count.

5. Global vs Rolling Shutter

Global shutter (Basler): All pixels are exposed simultaneously. No motion artifacts. Essential for fast-moving animals and multi-camera triangulation.

Rolling shutter (DJI, GoPro): Pixels are read row-by-row, causing skew/wobble with fast motion. Usually acceptable for behavioral tracking but can introduce subtle coordinate errors in pose estimation.

6. Top-Down vs Bottom-Up Mounting

Top-Down (Camera Above)

Most common for open-field, T-maze, and arena experiments. Camera mounted on ceiling or rig above the arena.

Pros: Unobstructed view, easy to set up, natural perspective for tracking.

Cons: Need sufficient ceiling height. Arena walls may occlude edges if camera is too close.

Typical distance: 60–200cm depending on arena size and FOV.

Bottom-Up (Camera Below)

Camera placed under a transparent floor (glass/acrylic). Used for paw tracking and gait analysis.

Pros: Clear view of paws and belly. No occlusion from arena walls.

Cons: Requires transparent floor. Floor scratches affect image quality. Limited to underside view.

Typical distance: 20–60cm (closer mounting possible).

7. Lens Distortion

Ultra-wide lenses (DJI, GoPro) exhibit significant barrel distortion — straight lines appear curved, especially near the edges. This affects measurement accuracy and tracking precision.

Distortion can be measured and corrected using a charuco calibration board (see the vibing.calibration module). Machine vision lenses (Basler) typically have much less distortion, especially at 6–12mm focal lengths.

Rule of thumb: If your arena fills >70% of the frame with an action camera, plan for distortion correction in your analysis pipeline.
